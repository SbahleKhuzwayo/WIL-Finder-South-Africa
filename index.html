    Guide: how WIL works

    What is WIL?

    Work-integrated learning is the workplace part of many diplomas and occupational qualifications. You finish your theory at college or university, then spend time in a real workplace so you can graduate. The exact length and rules depend on your institution and qualification.

    Where do I start?

    Ask your institution's WIL or Co-operative Education office what they require: the placement letter, logbook or portfolio, the number of months or hours, and any deadlines. Many institutions also keep a list of employers who have taken students before.

    How do I stand out?

    Apply to many places, apply early, and follow up politely after about a week. Keep your CV to one page, show what you can already do (projects, practical work), and match the advert's keywords honestly.

    Will I be paid?

    It varies. Some placements pay a stipend, some do not. Ask before you accept and check what your institution requires from the employer.

    How do I avoid scams?

    A real placement never costs you money. Do not pay "registration", "processing" or "uniform" fees to get a placement. Apply only through the organisation's own website or email, and check that the email address matches the organisation's real domain.

    Can't find advertised placements?

    Send a short enquiry email to the HR or training department, visit local businesses and municipal offices in person with your CV, and ask lecturers and classmates for leads. Use the email builder above.
